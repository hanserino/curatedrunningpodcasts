Is lactate threshold training the future of marathon prep?

About this episode

Earlier this month, on the streets of the Big Apple, Sweden’s Andreas Almgren stormed to a stunning victory at the New York City Marathon, running away from a loaded professional field in convincing fashion in his first-ever attempt at the marathon distance.

And while debuting with a victory at an Abbott World Marathon Major was already enough to set the running world abuzz, it was Almgren’s closely-documented preparation for the race that really piqued the interest of coaches, athletes and fans around the world.

Almgren’s ascent in recent years to the top of global distance running has been one that he attributes in large part to a near-religious adherence to lactate threshold training. The training model places a premium on controlled efforts guided by precise blood test data, and grew to prominence thanks largely to Norway’s Jakob Ingebrigtsen and his reign over middle-distance races on the track.

While the approach was quick to gain momentum among elite middle-distance athletes, namely those trying to catch up with Ingebrigtsen by copying the so-called Norwegian Method, Almgren’s breakout performance has had a dual effect, not only shifting attention from the method’s spiritual home in Norway to their its neighbour (Sweden), but also raising questions as to whether the approach first-pioneered for middle-distance events on the track might prove even more useful at longer distances, such as the marathon.

This week on The Shakeout Podcast, we're joined by two of the leading minds on lactate threshold training here in Canada: Olympian and Canadian record holder Matt Hughes and exercise physiologist Ollie Armstrong. Through their work as head coach and sports scientist The Canadian Sport Institute Pacific and Athletics Canada’s high performance hub in Victoria, the pair have been at the forefront of applying the methodology to some of Canada’s top athletes. They join the show this week to explain the science behind lactate threshold training, what it looks like in practice and how it is reshaping the way athletes are preparing to compete.
