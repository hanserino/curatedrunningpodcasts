About this episode

It didn't take more than half a second to say yes.

Paul James Johnson had just finished telling Mark about his fourth Cocodona 250 finish — four belt buckles, a thousand miles at that distance — and right there, mid-interview, invited him out to crew and pace. Mark showed up the day the race started. He didn't leave until everyone packed up and went home. Six days. Forty-four miles as pacer, from mile 190 to mile 234, in the dark, in the Arizona desert, with a man who already had 190 miles on his legs.

Something shifted out there. And this episode is what came out of it.

Episode 38 of Run Long After 60 is the first-ever self-supported episode — no guest, just Mark Vega: U.S. Army veteran, attorney, two-time stroke survivor, and ultrarunner. He reflects on what 37 extraordinary guests actually taught him, what he's changing about the show and his race calendar, and six lessons that kept showing up whether he was looking for them or not.

In this episode: why community isn't just the finish line — it's the six days around it; the trash we're leaving on our sacred ground and what we're going to do about it; the evolving why that took Mark from "can I do this after two strokes?" to something quieter and more durable; DNF as Data Not Failure and the five-lens framework he built after four of them; what Ray Mena's question about identity and Jim Glenn's Guinness World Record taught him about reinvention; and why Lion Caldwell, Bobby Keogh, and Pete Livingstone are proof that it is genuinely not too late.

Plus: the referral network that built this show, a full roll call of all 37 guests who made it possible, and a brief tease of something new coming out of the endurance community.

This is a transition episode. The show is changing. So is Mark.
