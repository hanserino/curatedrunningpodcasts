About this episode

Flash floods stopped the 2026 Moab 240, and Troy Eid and his pacer Barry Sackett spent the night at the Bridger Jack aid station with a crowd of stranded runners, cots, bivy sacks tearing in the wind, and volunteers who kept everyone fed and laughing until morning.

A few weeks later, Mark sat down with both of them. All three are lawyers, and they took on the question nobody in this sport likes to ask out loud: should a runner have to qualify before starting a 200-mile race? Or is that the runner’s call, and the runner’s risk, alone?

Troy, 62, has run nine 200-mile races and hosts his own show, Chasing the Cutoff. Barry, 57, runs every day, paced Troy from Indian Creek to Bridger Jack, and directs races of his own. They walk through what happened on the course, then through the questions it raised: how far you’ll be from help, who carries the risk, the runner or the race, how other races vet their entrants, and what a runner should have done before a mountain 200.

It isn’t a takedown of anyone. It’s two ways of running a race, open entry and gated entry, and what each one asks of the runner and of the race.

And for the 59-year-old who wants to sign up for Moab tomorrow, Troy has advice: lift weights, build the base, and maybe start with the Moab Marathon. Barry’s is simpler: find the races that are fun, with people you love.

This is Episode 1 of The 200-Mile Question. After a few more races, the three of us come back for Part 2.
